Pinning a reading

pinOnClick holds the hovered column and its tooltip in place when it is clicked, so the reading stays while you look away from the chart. With dimOthers beside it the rest of the plot stays down too, which is what makes the comparison survive the pointer leaving. Escape releases it, and so does a press anywhere outside the mark. The click output fires either way.

Column colour

Columns take the categorical palette in list order and wrap past the eighth, so dropping a point recolours everything after it; a point that sets color pins its own.

Readings of a different standing

A point that sets fill="hatched" keeps its colour and takes a striped texture over it, which separates a projection from a settled figure without spending a second hue on it. Texture is the encoding that survives greyscale, print and colour-vision deficiency, so it carries the distinction where a lighter tint of the same colour would not. The chart reads nothing into the flag: which columns are projections is a fact only the caller has.

More than one column per category

Hand data a list of NAMED SERIES instead of a flat list of points and every category carries one column per series, keyed by the legend under the plot. stack decides how they share the band: grouped stands them side by side, stacked puts each on top of the one before it, and normalized stacks then divides by the category's own total, so every band is full and the axis reads in shares. A flat list of points is still one series and still takes the whole band, so nothing about a single-series chart changes.

A series takes one colour for all of its marks, where a flat list colours each mark by its own position — the marks of a single-series chart ARE the categories, and the marks of a grouped one are not. A series-wide fill paints every one of its columns, so the pair below separates a settled figure from a committed one by texture as well as by hue. The chart reads nothing into either: what the two columns MEAN is the caller's fact.

Stacked, the band's full height is the category's total; normalized, it is always the whole axis and only the SHARES move. The boundary between two segments is a 2px gap in the surface colour rather than a drawn rule — a gap survives print and colour-vision deficiency, and it is the second encoding the palette's separation band asks for. The value printed beside a segment stays the reading even under normalized , where the axis is the only thing that switches to shares.

Columns and a line, on two scales

Two charts in one plot: a figure per month on the left axis, and the running total of those months on the right. They are different quantities at different magnitudes, so one axis cannot carry both - the total dwarfs every month it is made of, and on a shared scale the columns flatten to nothing. Each axis therefore takes the colour of the series measured against it, which is the only thing on the canvas that says which numbers a mark is read against.

There is no combo component. This is ds-chart - the container this chart is built inside - with a dsColumnSeries and a dsLineSeries projected into it. A series that sets axis: 'secondary' is measured against the second axis; secondaryAxisMode decides how that axis relates to the first, and 'aligned' , the default, hangs it on the first axis's own gridlines at a round step. The line is written after the columns because a projected mark paints in the order it is written, and a cumulative line belongs over the readings it sums. Two marks make it one comparison, so the pointer anywhere in a month names both readings in one panel over the taller of the two.

Reference lines and bands

thresholds draws a target, a budget or an SLA across the plot, behind the columns. Each entry is ONE number, so the two ends of a horizontal line cannot drift apart; dash picks how it is stroked, and to turns the same object into a band between two readings. A threshold also opens the value axis far enough to hold it, so a target above every column still lands on the plot, and it is named in the chart's generated description rather than being drawn and left unsaid.

What the tooltip points at

The box that catches the pointer is the full-height category cell, not the column — that is what keeps a short column reachable. tooltipAnchor decides which of the two the panel points at: 'mark', the default, puts it over the column, and 'cell' puts it at the top of the cell, which is where it lands if you anchor to the box rather than the mark. pointClicked fires on click and on Enter whether or not interactive is set.

Data table

none , the default, renders no table at all; toggle puts one behind a closed disclosure; visible renders it open. The table is the non-visual route to the numbers, so none belongs only where the same figures are already in a table on the page.

Stagger

A mark that leaves finishes leaving before a mark that arrives starts, so a vanishing column and a new one are never on screen together. staggerMs spaces the arrivals out — 0 , the default, is no stagger at all, and 40 is the documented step. The total delay is capped at 300ms, so a long series does not crawl in. Edit the data below to watch it. Under prefers-reduced-motion every duration is zero and each column lands straight on its final size.
