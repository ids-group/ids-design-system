Annotations

annotations pins a label over one reading, so a peak reads in a screenshot or a printed page with no pointer. Each names a categoryIndex and, optionally, the seriesId it belongs to — left out, the first plotted series. A label near an edge anchors to that edge instead of centring, and one whose series is hidden draws nothing. They are aria-hidden: the annex table already carries every reading.

Missing readings

A null reading is a hole, not a zero, and gaps decides what it draws: break , the default, stops the line and picks it up after; zero draws through the baseline. Any non-finite value takes the same route, so an upstream NaN lands here too.

Second axis · rounded (default)

A series in other units sets axis: 'secondary' and is measured against the right-hand scale; yAxisMode reconciles the two. Rounded keeps one set of gridlines and rounds the secondary step up, so that line stops short of the top.

Area fill

area: true fills the series down to its axis baseline, which asserts that zero means something for it — wrong for a rate or an index. Fill one series at most: two translucent fills overlap into a third quantity that is not there.

Long series

At most twelve category labels are drawn whatever the length — the chart keeps every nth and drops the rest — while the line still carries every reading.

Tick count

tickCount asks for roughly that many gridlines on the value axis. It is a request rather than a count: the scale rounds to a step a reader can do arithmetic with, so five ticks over a domain of 0–2,740 become 0/1K/2K/3K rather than five evenly-spaced odd numbers.

Pinning the value axis

A panel that opens one subject at a time derives a different axis for each, so two plots with the same gentle wiggle can be four times apart in value with nothing on screen saying so. max pins the axis instead, which is what makes two renders comparable. Null, the default, keeps the derived axis. maxSecondary does the same for the axis a series reaches with axis: 'secondary' .

derived axis · a quiet series fills the plot
max = 5000 · the same data, read against a fixed ceiling

A maximum below the data does not clip it — the larger of the two wins, because hiding a reading is worse than more headroom than you asked for.

Stagger

staggerMs spaces the series' arrivals out, so a chart with several lines draws them one after another rather than all at once. 0, the default, means no stagger.

Nothing left to draw

A filtering legend can switch every series off, which is a different state from having no data at all — the reader did it, and can undo it. noVisibleSeriesText is what the plot says then; it keeps the plot's height either way, so the page does not jump.

Interactive

interactive makes every reading focusable and clickable, and grows an invisible marker on a series drawn without one so there is something to land on. The hit area is a transparent 24px stroke on a fine pointer and 44px on a coarse one — a 4px dot is not a touch target. Tab to a marker and the tooltip names that series alone; hover the plot and it names every series at the category. A tap latches the tooltip open until the next tap outside. pointClicked fires whether or not the flag is set. dimOthers is a separate opt-in, off by default on all five charts: fading the rest costs the separation between the faded marks, so it buys loudness for one reading and pays for it with the others.

tooltipAnchor decides where the panel sits: 'point' , the default, pins it over the highest reading in the category, so it stays still while the pointer travels within one band; 'pointer' follows the cursor instead.

Patterns

patterns gives every series a dash of its own, in palette-slot order, so the lines are told apart by shape as well as by colour — WCAG technique G111. Slot 1 stays solid, so a single-series chart is unchanged by turning it on. Off by default: the shipped palette clears the colour-blind separation threshold on its own, and this is for monochrome print, a retuned palette, or a reader who asks for it.

patterns = false (default)

What a legend control does

legendMode picks the question the legend answers. isolate, the default, shows one series and hides the rest — one action instead of one per series, which is what a reader of a crowded chart wants. toggle hides one and leaves the rest, for taking a dominant series out so the others can be read at a usable scale. They are different controls, not two styles of one: isolate is a single choice among many and renders as radios with an All option, toggle renders as checkboxes.

legendMode = isolate (default)
legendMode = toggle

Filtering legend

legendFilters turns each legend entry into a checkbox that switches its series off, and hiddenSeries is two-way, so a page can drive it from a URL parameter. The value axis is scaled from every series, hidden or not, unless rescaleOnHide says otherwise — a silent rescale turns a period-over-period comparison into a different claim. Hovering an entry dims the other series. Switch them all off and the plot is replaced by an explanation rather than an empty grid.

rescaleOnHide = false (default)
