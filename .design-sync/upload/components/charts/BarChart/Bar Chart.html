Horizontal bars: each row draws its value as a share of the longest row.

Default

Rows keep the order you pass them — the chart never sorts. Track length is a share of the largest value in the set, not of a total, so the longest row always fills its track.

Segmented rows

A row that carries segments divides its bar into parts, so the bar still says how the row compares with the rest of the chart and the parts say what it is made of — how one department spends its budget, on a bar whose length is still that department's share of the largest. The parts are sized against their own sum and always fill the bar: a set that does not add up to the row's value is a data error, and a gap left to show it would read as one more category. Segments are categories, so they take the categorical palette in order; the row's own colour is unused. Each part reaches the data table as its own row under the one it belongs to. The parts carry no text of their own, so draw a key beside the chart from the same --ds-chart-series-N-color values, in the order the segments are passed.

A second reading per row

A row often encodes a ratio and the bar can only draw one of its two numbers. detail prints the other under the label, in the row's own words — it is already formatted, because the row knows what its second number means and the chart does not. It reaches the data table as well.

Share of the total

partOfWhole says the rows add up to something a reader can name — a budget, a headcount, a quarter's revenue. Only then does the chart state each row's share of that total, on the tooltip's second line and as a Share of total column in the data table. It is off by default because rows that are a funnel or a time series sum to a number nobody can point at, and a percentage of that is arithmetic dressed as a fact. Note that the percentage printed beside each row is a different figure: that one is the share of the LARGEST row.

Pinning a reading

pinOnClick holds the clicked row and its tooltip in place, so the reading is still on screen after the pointer has gone. A pin glyph marks which row is held, and a Reset appears in the corner beside it. Esc releases it too, and so does clicking the row again.

Edit data

Percent beside the value

showPercent is on by default and prints each row's share of the LARGEST row beside its value — a quick read of how the rows compare to the biggest one. Turn it off where the rows are too narrow to hold a second figure, or where it would be confused with partOfWhole , which is a share of the sum rather than of the maximum. A row can also carry its own percent , which is printed instead of the computed one.

Interaction

interactive turns on the pointer cursor and the hover tint, and makes each row a tab stop. rowClicked fires whether or not the flag is set. dimOthers fades the rows that are not under the pointer — off by default, because the fade costs the separation BETWEEN the faded rows.
