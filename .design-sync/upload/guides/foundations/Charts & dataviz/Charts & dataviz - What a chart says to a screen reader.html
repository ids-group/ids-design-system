Charts & dataviz - what a chart says to a screen reader

A path and a set of arcs describe nothing one mark at a time, so a chart does not expose its marks — it carries ONE generated sentence: its caption, then each series with its readings, then a pointer to the annex table when one is rendered. The plot wears that sentence as its accessible name and the marks are closed off behind it. caption is the visible title and feeds that sentence; the table beneath carries the numbers themselves.

summary replaces the generated sentence outright, on all five charts. Reach for it where the generated one is true and useless — forty categories read out one by one, or a plot whose point is a shape rather than its values ("Conversion falls sharply after the third step"). Leave it alone otherwise: the generated sentence follows the data, and a hand-written one goes stale the moment the numbers change.
