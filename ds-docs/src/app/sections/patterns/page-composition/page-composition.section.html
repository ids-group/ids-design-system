Four regions, and three of them are components

Nothing on a page sits outside one of these. The sidenav is reserved OUTSIDE the page grid; the topbar is global, not per-page. Both are drawn dashed here for that reason.

ds-page-footer optional

The header slots are a reading order, not a set

[breadcrumbs] above the title, [title-badge] beside it, [meta] under it, [actions] right-aligned on the title line, [tabs] last so the tab row sits on the header's bottom edge. Putting page actions anywhere else — a floating button, a row above the header — breaks the one place a user has learned to look.

Three placement rules that settle most arguments

  • Filters sit directly above the data they filter — between the header and the content, full width, with no wrapper that could clip the overflow popover. Not in a sidebar, not inside the table's own card. The 9+3 split is what a genuine filter RAIL is for, and it is reserved, not the default.
  • One primary per region, once — one in the page header, one per card, one in a modal's actions. A second primary in the same region makes neither primary. Secondary work takes outline , dismissive work takes ghost .
  • Supporting information goes under, never beside — hints, counts and timestamps sit under the thing they describe at --space-1 . Beside is reserved for actions. Never a tooltip: it is hover-only, so it cannot carry anything the user needs in order to act.

When content belongs in a card

A card earns its border when what is inside is a UNIT that could be moved, collapsed or repeated — a record summary, a KPI tile, one form section. It does not earn one for "this page has content": a full-width table already has its own frame, and a card around it double-borders. The default is no card; reach for one when the boundary means something.
