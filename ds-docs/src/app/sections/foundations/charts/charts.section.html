Chart palettes and axis tokens, and the behaviour every chart shares: states, tooltips, pinning, frame, annex table and app-wide defaults.

Categorical palette (8)

Hand-tuned for perceptual distance, not ramp position. Every hue clears 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). Slot 8 is the neutral "rest" / "other" bucket. A chart of one series takes --ds-chart-series-default-color , with --ds-chart-series-accent-color for the row to land on.

@for (n of slots; track n) {
--ds-chart-series-{{ n }}-color
}

Ramp and heat

--ds-chart-ramp-N-color is one hue at mid lightness for the ordered stages of one quantity (funnel stages). --ds-chart-heat-N-color spans the full lightness range, for a grid read from the step between cells (heat map).

@for (n of slots; track n) {
--ds-chart-ramp-{{ n }}-color
}
@for (n of slots; track n) {
--ds-chart-heat-{{ n }}-color
}

Sequential ramp

Single-hue, light → dark. Use for ordinal magnitudes (e.g. utilisation %, days-until-deadline). No component reads the sequential or divergent ramps and they have no --ds-chart-* override: they are for hand-drawn visuals.

@for (n of seqs; track n) {
--chart-sequential-{{ n }}
}

Divergent ramp

Red ← neutral → green for signed values where 0 is neutral.

@for (n of divs; track n) {
{{ n > 0 ? '+' + n : n }}
}

How a chart arrives

Marks arrive in stages. Columns, bars and waterfall steps grow out of their baseline over --ds-motion-chart-mark-grow-duration (600ms), a ring turns in and a line draws on over --ds-motion-chart-mark-sweep-duration (600ms), and value labels and markers follow over --ds-motion-chart-mark-detail-duration (180ms). A mark that fades or settles in place takes --ds-motion-chart-mark-enter-duration (400ms). A series' arrivals spread across 260ms, however many marks it has. Under reduced motion every mark appears at once.

Empty, loading and error states

Every chart draws these itself: empty data shows emptyText , [loading] a skeleton, and [error] errorText . Unset copy comes from DS_INTL.chart .

Reading a value without keeping the pointer still

An inert tooltip cannot be read at high zoom, cannot have its number selected, and cannot be cleared without moving the pointer somewhere else. WCAG 1.4.13 asks for all three, so every chart panel here takes the pointer and Esc closes it. Once cleared it stays cleared until you visit another mark, so pressing Esc to get the panel off the screen actually gets it off the screen.

A chart answers the pointer at once: the tooltip, the highlight and the dimming show the moment it reaches a mark. DS_CONFIG.chart.hoverDelayMs makes it wait for a resting pointer instead; once the chart has answered, the next mark answers at once until the pointer leaves the chart. Focus, a tap and a click that pins answer at once.

One panel serves the whole chart. Moving from one mark to the next glides it there rather than closing it and opening another, and it goes once, tooltipLingerMs after the pointer leaves: 500 by default, which is what lets the pointer cross the gap between two marks and find the same panel. Set it to 0 to take it away at once, per chart or on DS_CONFIG.chart.tooltipLingerMs . A scroll closes a panel that follows the pointer; a pinned one moves with its mark.

Each reading carries its series' colour dot when there are two to four readings; with one or five-plus the labels carry the series alone. A dot gets a ring when it is below 1.5:1 against the panel. tooltipSwatches (per chart or on DS_CONFIG.chart ) forces dots on or off; tooltip="none" removes the panel.

tooltipSwatches unset (default)
tooltipSwatches = false
tooltip = none

The panel's colours are --ds-chart-tooltip-bg-color , -text-color and -border-color . Set them on the theme for every chart, or on one chart: the panel is portaled out to the page, and the chart copies its own values onto it when it opens.

A click on a mark holds it by default on every chart: the mark keeps its tooltip, its highlight and, with dimOthers , the dimming of the rest. A pin disc marks it, and every chart, a composed ds-chart included, shows an Unpin control at its top end. A second click, Esc or a press elsewhere releases it; the click output fires either way. Enter and Space on a focused mark fire the click output and never pin. pinOnClick="false" (per chart, ds-chart included) or DS_CONFIG.chart.pinOnClick: false turns holding off; a touch tap still holds the mark, since touch has no hover. On ds-line-chart a press holds the whole category under the cursor.

Custom tooltip content

ds-bar-chart , ds-column-chart , ds-funnel-chart , ds-line-chart and ds-pie-chart read a projected <ng-template #dsChartTooltip> as the panel's body. It receives a ChartTooltipContext : category (empty where the marks are the categories) and entries, each with label , value , formatted , color and an optional detail .

{{ context.category }} @for (entry of context.entries; track entry.label) { {{ entry.label }}: {{ entry.formatted }} } 
<ds-line-chart [categories]="months" [series]="series">
  <ng-template #dsChartTooltip let-context>
    <strong>{{ context.category }}</strong>
    @for (entry of context.entries; track entry.label) {
      <span>{{ entry.label }}: {{ entry.formatted }}</span>
    }
  </ng-template>
</ds-line-chart>

The frame a chart draws around itself

A chart draws its own bordered, padded box by default so its plot, legend and annex table do not run into neighbours. Inside a card, turn it off with frame="none" per chart or DS_CONFIG.chart.frame per app.

frame = auto (default)
frame = none

The --ds-chart-frame-* tokens still size the frame when it is on. Reaching for them to switch it OFF is the thing the input replaces: a rule against a component's own chrome from a global sheet.

Background

--ds-chart-bg-color is what sits behind the marks. The frame is painted in it, and so is every cut a chart makes between two marks: the band a line rides on, the seam between segments, hatching, the halo round a label or a focused mark. On a card of another colour with frame="none" , set it to the card's colour and the cuts disappear into it. --ds-chart-plot-bg-color paints only the rectangle inside the axes; unset, it is transparent.

grey card, background unset
--ds-chart-bg-color = the card
--ds-chart-plot-bg-color set

The annex table

dataTable gives the numbers behind a plot a route that is not a colour or a length. none , the default, draws no table; toggle puts it behind a closed disclosure; visible renders it open. Set it per chart, or on DS_CONFIG.chart.dataTable for an app that wants the annex everywhere. A heat map carries its table always, because colour is the only channel carrying its magnitude.

dataTable = none (default)
dataTable = toggle
dataTable = visible

What a chart says to a screen reader

A path and a set of arcs describe nothing one mark at a time, so a chart does not expose its marks — it carries ONE generated sentence: its caption, then each series with its readings, then a pointer to the annex table when one is rendered. The plot wears that sentence as its accessible name and the marks are closed off behind it. caption is the visible title and feeds that sentence; the table beneath carries the numbers themselves.

summary replaces the generated sentence outright, on all nine charts; ds-sparkline has no generated sentence and takes its name from label. Reach for it where the generated one is true and useless — forty categories read out one by one, or a plot whose point is a shape rather than its values ("Conversion falls sharply after the third step"). Leave it alone otherwise: the generated sentence follows the data, and a hand-written one goes stale the moment the numbers change.

App-wide chart defaults

Eight app-wide decisions resolve through DS_CONFIG.chart : valueFormat , dataTable , frame , tooltipLingerMs , tooltipSwatches , pinOnClick , hoverDelayMs (config only, no per-chart input) and rendering (how charts build on a long page, below). Precedence is input, then config, then built-in default. Empty and error copy lives in DS_INTL.chart ; the number locale is DsConfig.locale , then LOCALE_ID . 

providers: [
  provideDsConfig({
    locale: 'de-DE',
    chart: {
      dataTable: 'visible',
      frame: 'none',
      tooltipLingerMs: 140,
      pinOnClick: false,
      hoverDelayMs: 0,
    },
  }),
  provideDsIntl({ chart: { empty: 'Нет данных', error: 'Не удалось загрузить' } }),
]

A page with dozens of charts

Every chart draws the moment it is created, which is right for a screen with a few of them: nothing waits and nothing appears late. A dashboard or a report with dozens turns on rendering.deferUntilVisible , and each chart then holds its place with a still placeholder of its own shape until it comes within visibleMargin of the viewport. While the page is idle the charts within buildAheadMargin are built ahead, so the next scroll finds them ready, and a built chart that scrolls away costs no style, layout or paint until it returns. Until a deferred chart is built a screen reader meets its caption and nothing else, so leave it off where every chart has to be readable from the first moment. 

providers: [
  provideDsConfig({
    chart: {
      rendering: {
        deferUntilVisible: true,
        visibleMargin: '10% 0px',
        buildAheadWhileIdle: true,
        buildAheadMargin: '100% 0px',
        frameBudgetMs: 8,
        skipWhileOffscreen: true,
      },
    },
  }),
]
