Describe the shape and the rules in the component, then point each control at a field. The DS side is one attribute — [formField] — and it comes from @angular/forms/signals , not from this library. That is the point: a DS control participates because it exposes value as a model() , which is the entire FormValueControl contract.

Type an invalid address and leave the field: the message appears only after the field is touched, the border turns, aria-invalid flips, and the button stays disabled until the form is valid. None of that is wired in the template.

Error text comes from the validator

A field knows it is invalid, but only your validator knows what to say. Without a message the field turns red with no words — deliberately, since the alternative is the library inventing copy for your domain. Two ways to supply it: per rule, as required(path.email, { message: '…' }) , or once per app for the built-in kinds:

In dev, a field that is invalid with no resolvable message logs a warning naming the error kinds it could not resolve, so this fails loudly rather than silently.

Reactive and template-driven forms still work

Nothing here replaces anything. Every control still provides a ControlValueAccessor , so formControlName and ngModel bind exactly as before, and the same message row serves both. A given field is driven by one form system at a time, so the two cannot disagree.
