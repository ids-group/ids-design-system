The scaffold for pre-login screens; the counterpart to ds-app-layout .

Parts: brand lockup, optional tagline, form card, support line. The brand row takes the sidenav's brand / logoLetter / [dsBrandName] contract; the tile has its own --ds-auth-layout-mark-* hooks; retint both.

A real logo goes into [brand] (sized by --ds-auth-layout-brand-max-height ). It does not hide the tile: leave brand / logoLetter unset.

provideDsConfig({ brand: { name, logoLetter, tagline } }) serves this layout and ds-sidenav , per field; inputs beat the config. brand="" drops the wordmark, but a configured logoLetter still paints the tile; clear both for no brand row. The config has no accent: project [dsBrandName] for a two-tone wordmark; the projection wins over both.

The default slot fills the card, unstyled. The support line goes into [dsAuthSupport] : the contentinfo landmark when this layout is the page, a plain footer when nested in another layout's main; omit it and the footer collapses. The card (an article) is marked main by default; nested in another layout's main , as here, set landmark="none" .

On a short screen the chrome compresses, not the card

Block padding, column gap and masthead gap clamp against svh , reaching full size at 800px of viewport height (many laptops have less); type and tile stay fixed. The clamps read the real viewport: shorten the window to see them.

svh , not dvh , so the column does not re-space as a mobile URL bar hides. A fixed --ds-auth-layout-padding-block or -gap opts that dimension out.

A too-tall card scrolls; the chrome stays in flow (no position: absolute ), never overlapping it.

Why it centres with auto margins

The column centres with auto margins, not align-items: center , so in a fixed-height parent (--ds-auth-layout-min-height: 100%) a too-tall form pins to the top padding and scrolls rather than landing above the scroll origin. The demos lower that token.

The notch needs one line from YOUR app

The layout pads by env(safe-area-inset-*) to clear a landscape notch; ds-bottom-sheet does the same for the home indicator. The insets are 0 until the host document opts in via the viewport meta tag in your index.html . 

<!-- your index.html -->
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover" />

Without it every inset reads 0. This site sets it.
