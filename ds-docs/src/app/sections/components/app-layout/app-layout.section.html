Opening the rail from your own chrome

Below lg the rail is an overlay opened from your topbar. On a native button , add dsRailTrigger : it toggles the rail and syncs aria-expanded / aria-controls . Not on a ds-icon-button (the attributes land on the wrapper); bind its forwarding inputs off a <ds-app-layout #layout> ref: 

<ds-icon-button
  icon="menu"
  ariaLabel="Open navigation"
  [ariaExpanded]="layout.railOverlay() ? layout.railOpen() : null"
  [ariaControls]="layout.railOverlay() ? layout.railId : null"
  (pressed)="layout.railOpen.set(!layout.railOpen())" />

Both attributes drop while the rail is not an overlay; aria-expanded="false" would misstate a visible rail.

[(railOpen)] is the open state. railLabel names the overlay dialog (default DS_INTL.appLayout.railLabel ). The open overlay traps focus; Escape or a scrim click closes it and refocuses the trigger. Requires the router (provideRouter).

Returning the reader to where they were

[restoreScroll]="true" remembers scroll per route and restores it only on history traversal (popstate: back, forward, mouse side button, Alt+←, edge swipe), reload, or navigation with state: {{ '{' }} dsRestoreScroll: true {{ '}' }} ; a new screen starts at the top. Wheel, touch or key input cancels a pending restore. These docs use it.

scrollReset sets when content returns to the top: 'path' (default) on path change only, so search, sort and paging keep the place; 'always' also on query/fragment-only navigation (server-paged lists); 'never' leaves it to the screen, via AppLayoutScrollService.scrollToTop() .

AppLayoutNavigationService.pathChanged answers "did we go elsewhere?": NavigationEnd , path only (no query or fragment), de-duplicated, skipping navigations with state: {{ '{' }} dsSamePage: true {{ '}' }} . Inject it; do not derive your own.

The offset applies once content is tall enough, watched by a ResizeObserver and capped at restoreScrollTimeout , so a list with 8 of 40 rows loaded still lands close. A page that sets its own scroll (a virtualizer picks rows BEFORE it can scroll) should take the offset itself: inject(AppLayoutScrollService).consumePendingOffset() .

The rail footer on its own

<ds-sidenav-footer> is the rail part you configure rather than compose: profile row, alert bell, collapse handle. [collapsed] switches between the two states below. Width comes from the sidenav; outside one, give it a frame.

The footer only emits collapse (collapseToggled) and sign-out (signOutSelected); the parent acts, so the unbound collapse row here does nothing. A null searchProvider or alertItems makes that row inert; an empty profileRoute makes "Your profile" a no-op.
