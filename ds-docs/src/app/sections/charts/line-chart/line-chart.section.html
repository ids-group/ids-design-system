One or more series plotted over a shared set of categories, with an optional second y axis for a series in different units.

Default

Series share one set of categories and take categorical palette colours in the order you pass them.

Annotations

annotations pins a label over one reading, so a peak reads in a screenshot or a printed page with no pointer. Each names a categoryIndex and, optionally, the seriesId it belongs to — left out, the first plotted series. A label near an edge anchors to that edge instead of centring, and one whose series is hidden draws nothing. They are aria-hidden: the annex table already carries every reading.

Composition over time

Separate lines answer "how is each one doing"; a stack answers "what is the total made of", which is a different question and the one a mix of tiers usually asks. stack decides which: stacked starts each series where the one below it ended, and normalized divides by the category's own total, so every month is full and the axis reads in shares. Only a series with area fills; the lines themselves still sit on the running total either way. Stacking runs per AXIS, so a series on the secondary axis never piles onto one measured against the primary.

Between the readings

A line drawn between two readings is a claim about what happened in between, and curve is where that claim is made. linear , the default, asserts nothing the data does not say. step holds each reading until the next, which is what a quantity that CHANGES at a moment actually did - a price, a headcount, a rate. smooth is a monotone cubic, so it never draws a peak or a trough the readings do not contain; a Catmull-Rom would invent one on either side of the plateau below. A filled series follows the same curve, so the fill and its line share one edge.

linear (default)
step
smooth

Missing readings

A null reading is a hole, not a zero, and gaps decides what it draws: break , the default, stops the line and picks it up after; zero draws through the baseline. Any non-finite value takes the same route, so an upstream NaN lands here too.

break (default)
zero

Where two lines cross is an artefact

Two scales on one plot buy comparability and charge for it. Below are the SAME six months twice, with only the right axis's ceiling moved: the lines cross between February and March on the left and never meet on the right. It is a property of the scale that was chosen, not a fact in the data: no reading changed between the two pictures.

All four modes stay for that reason - none of them removes the artefact, so removing a mode would only hide the choice. What the chart does instead is say which numbers a line is read against: each axis takes the colour of the series measured against it, and yAxisLabel and secondaryAxisLabel name the units. Without both a reader is comparing two quantities that may not be comparable at all.

Second axis · aligned (default)

A series in other units sets axis: 'secondary' and is measured against the right-hand scale; yAxisMode reconciles the two. Rounded keeps one set of gridlines and rounds the secondary step up, so that line stops short of the top.

Second axis · exact

The secondary step is taken straight from the peak, so that series uses the full height and its labels are no longer round numbers.

Second axis · dual-grid

Each axis is niced on its own and both series use the full height, at the price of two sets of gridlines — the secondary's are dashed so the right-hand labels can be told apart.

Second axis · split

Two plots over a shared category axis, labelled under the lower one only: nothing overlaps, but the two shapes can no longer be read against each other.

Markers

Turn showMarkers off once the readings are dense enough that the markers merge into a band.

Outline

outline lays each line and its markers on a band of the surface colour, so a line crossing a gridline or another line stays unbroken. It is on by default; turn it off on a plot with no gridlines or where the lines never cross, and the band only separates them from the ground.

outline = true (default)
outline = false

Area fill

area: true fills the series down to its axis baseline, which asserts that zero means something for it — wrong for a rate or an index. Fill one series at most: two translucent fills overlap into a third quantity that is not there.

Long series

At most twelve category labels are drawn whatever the length — the chart keeps every nth and drops the rest — while the line still carries every reading.

Tick count

tickCount asks for roughly that many gridlines on the value axis. It is a request rather than a count: the scale rounds to a step a reader can do arithmetic with, so five ticks over a domain of 0–2,740 become 0/1K/2K/3K rather than five evenly-spaced odd numbers.

tickCount = 5 (default)
tickCount = 3

Pinning the value axis

A panel that opens one subject at a time derives a different axis for each, so two plots with the same gentle wiggle can be four times apart in value with nothing on screen saying so. max pins the axis instead, which is what makes two renders comparable. Null, the default, keeps the derived axis. maxSecondary does the same for the axis a series reaches with axis: 'secondary' .

derived axis · a quiet series fills the plot
max = 5000 · the same data, read against a fixed ceiling

A maximum below the data does not clip it — the larger of the two wins, because hiding a reading is worse than more headroom than you asked for.

Stagger

staggerMs spaces the series' arrivals out, so a chart with several lines draws them one after another. 40 is the default and 0 draws them all at once. A solid line draws on and a dashed one fades in; an area fades in under its line, and the markers settle once the line has arrived. The whole spread fits inside 260ms.

Nothing left to draw

A filtering legend can switch every series off, which is a different state from having no data at all — the reader did it, and can undo it. noVisibleSeriesText is what the plot says then; it keeps the plot's height either way, so the page does not jump. The loading skeleton and the empty and error boxes take --ds-line-chart-placeholder-height when it is set, so they can be sized apart from the plot, which reads only --ds-line-chart-plot-height .

Data table

none , the default, renders no table at all; toggle puts one behind a closed disclosure; visible renders it open. The table is the non-visual route to the numbers, so none belongs only where the same figures are already in a table on the page.

Interactive

interactive makes every reading focusable and clickable, and grows an invisible marker on a series drawn without one so there is something to land on. The hit area is a transparent 24px stroke on a fine pointer and 44px on a coarse one — a 4px dot is not a touch target. Tab to a marker and the tooltip names that series alone; hover the plot and it names every series at the category. A tap holds the tooltip open: with the default pinOnClick a second tap on the same category lets go, and a tap outside always does. pointClicked fires whether or not the flag is set. dimOthers is a separate opt-in, off by default on every chart: fading the rest costs the separation between the faded marks, so it buys loudness for one reading and pays for it with the others.

A press on the plot holds the category under the cursor, with its band, its panel and a pin disc at the foot of the band, until a second press, Esc or a press elsewhere lets go. An Unpin control appears at the top end of the chart, on the caption's line. A marker does not pin on its own: Enter and Space on a focused marker fire pointClicked and hold nothing. Set pinOnClick="false" to keep the cursor moving with the pointer, or DS_CONFIG.chart.pinOnClick: false for every chart in the app.

tooltipAnchor decides where the panel sits: 'point' , the default, pins it over the highest reading in the category, so it stays still while the pointer travels within one band; 'pointer' follows the cursor instead.

Patterns

patterns gives every series a dash of its own, in palette-slot order, so the lines are told apart by shape as well as by colour — WCAG technique G111. Slot 1 stays solid, so a single-series chart is unchanged by turning it on. Off by default: the shipped palette clears the colour-blind separation threshold on its own, and this is for monochrome print, a retuned palette, or a reader who asks for it.

patterns = false (default)

What a legend control does

legendMode picks the question the legend answers. isolate, the default, shows one series and hides the rest — one action instead of one per series, which is what a reader of a crowded chart wants. toggle hides one and leaves the rest, for taking a dominant series out so the others can be read at a usable scale. They are different controls, not two styles of one: isolate is a single choice among many and renders as radios with an All option, toggle renders as checkboxes.

legendMode = isolate (default), dimOthers
legendMode = toggle

Filtering legend

legendFilters turns each legend entry into a checkbox that switches its series off, and hiddenSeries is two-way, so a page can drive it from a URL parameter. The value axis is scaled from every series, hidden or not, unless rescaleOnHide says otherwise — a silent rescale turns a period-over-period comparison into a different claim. Hovering an entry fades the other series once the pointer has rested on it, and focusing one fades them at once, whether or not dimOthers is set. Switch them all off and the plot is replaced by an explanation rather than an empty grid.

rescaleOnHide = false (default)
