Vertical columns for a short series read left to right — months, stages, teams.

Default

Columns keep the order you pass them and share one axis niced to a round step, which is why the tallest column usually stops short of the top gridline.

Tick count

tickCount is a hint: ticks snap to a round step, so an axis asked for three ends up with five and one asked for ten stops at eight.

Pinning a reading

A click holds the column and its tooltip, with a pin disc on it and an Unpin control on the caption's line. Escape, a second click or a press outside releases it, and pointClicked fires either way. dimOthers fades every column but the one the chart answers (hovered after the pointer rests, focused, or pinned). pinOnClick="false" keeps columns unheld.

Column values

Turn showValues off where the columns are too narrow to hold a figure, or where an open annex table already prints the same numbers.

Column colour

Columns take the categorical palette in list order and wrap past the eighth, so dropping a point recolours everything after it; a point that sets color pins its own.

Readings of a different standing

A point that sets fill="hatched" on a point keeps its colour under a striped texture, separating a projection from a settled figure in greyscale, print and colour-vision deficiency. The chart infers nothing: the caller marks which points are projections.

More than one column per category

Hand data a list of NAMED SERIES instead of a flat list of points and every category carries one column per series, keyed by the legend under the plot; hovering or focusing a legend item singles its series out. stack decides how they share the band: 'none' (default) and grouped stand them side by side, stacked puts each on top of the one before it, and normalized stacks then divides by the category's own total, so every band is full and the axis reads in shares. A flat list of points is still one series and still takes the whole band, so nothing about a single-series chart changes.

A series takes one colour for all of its marks, where a flat list colours each mark by its own position — the marks of a single-series chart ARE the categories, and the marks of a grouped one are not. A series-wide fill paints every one of its columns, so the pair below separates a settled figure from a committed one by texture as well as by hue. The chart reads nothing into either: what the two columns MEAN is the caller's fact.

Stacked, the band's full height is the category's total; normalized, it is always the whole axis and only the SHARES move. The boundary between two segments is a 2px gap in the surface colour rather than a drawn rule — a gap survives print and colour-vision deficiency, and it is the second encoding the palette's separation band asks for. The value printed beside a segment stays the reading even under normalized , where the axis is the only thing that switches to shares.

Columns and a line, on two scales

A figure per month on the left axis and its running total on the right: one axis cannot carry both, so each axis takes the colour of the series read against it.

There is no combo component: this is ds-chart with a dsColumnSeries and a dsLineSeries projected in. A series with axis: 'secondary' reads the second axis, related to the first by secondaryAxisMode (default 'aligned' , on the first axis's gridlines at a round step). Marks paint in source order, so the line is written after the columns; with two marks the pointer anywhere in a month names both readings in one panel.

Reference lines and bands

thresholds draws a target, budget or SLA behind the columns. Each entry is one value ; dash sets its stroke and to turns it into a band between two readings. A threshold widens the value axis to stay on the plot and is named in the generated description.

What the tooltip points at

The box that catches the pointer is the column's full-height share of its category's step, not the drawn column, which keeps a short column reachable and leaves no ground between two columns that answers nothing. tooltipAnchor decides which of the two the panel points at: 'mark', the default, puts it over the column, and 'cell' puts it at the top of the cell, which is where it lands if you anchor to the box rather than the mark. pointClicked fires on every click whether or not interactive is set; Enter and Space fire it on an interactive column, and never pin. An interactive chart is one tab stop: arrow keys move between columns (wrapping), Home and End jump to the ends.

Sizing

Geometry is set by tokens, not inputs: --ds-column-chart-plot-height (200px), --ds-column-chart-axis-width (40px) and --ds-column-chart-value-space are read back as numbers, so they take a px length only; --ds-column-chart-column-width is a percentage of the slot (80%). --ds-column-chart-column-max-width , --ds-column-chart-column-gap , --ds-column-chart-cap-radius and --ds-column-chart-grid-line-color finish the set.

Data table

none , the default, renders no table at all; toggle puts one behind a closed disclosure; visible renders it open. The table is the non-visual route to the numbers, so none belongs only where the same figures are already in a table on the page.

Stagger

A mark that leaves finishes leaving before a mark that arrives starts, so a vanishing column and a new one are never on screen together. staggerMs spaces the arrivals out. 40 is the default and 0 turns it off. The whole spread fits inside 260ms, so a long series narrows the step instead of crawling in. Edit the data below to watch it. Under prefers-reduced-motion every duration is zero and each column lands straight on its final size.

staggerMs = 0
staggerMs = 40 (default)
