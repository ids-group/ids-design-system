Horizontal bars: each row draws its value as a share of the longest row.

Default

Rows keep the order you pass them — the chart never sorts. Track length is a share of the largest value in the set, not of a total, so the longest row always fills its track.

Floating bars

A row's value can be a [low, high] pair instead of one number, and the bar then spans between its own two ends rather than from zero. It is what compares a duration and its PLACEMENT at once - a phase, a shift, a min-to-max - which a bar from zero cannot do. The ends may be written either way round.

Target and bands

target draws a tick across the bar at one reading - a goal, a budget, last period - and ranges paints qualitative bands behind it, given as their upper bounds. Together they are the bullet chart, which is a bar with two extras rather than a chart type of its own. The bands take one neutral hue at rising opacity: a band is a backdrop and the bar is the reading, so a second palette behind them would compete with the bars.

Row colour

Every row takes the same single-series colour; a row that sets color opts out.

Segmented rows

A row that carries segments divides its bar into parts, so the bar still says how the row compares with the rest of the chart and the parts say what it is made of — how one department spends its budget, on a bar whose length is still that department's share of the largest. The parts are sized against their own sum and always fill the bar: a set that does not add up to the row's value is a data error, and a gap left to show it would read as one more category. Segments are categories, so they take the categorical palette in order; the row's own colour is unused. Each part reaches the data table as its own row under the one it belongs to. The parts carry no text of their own, so draw a key beside the chart from the same --ds-chart-series-N-color values, in the order the segments are passed.

    @for (part of segmentKey; track part.label) {
  • {{ part.label }}
    • }

Singling out a segment

highlighted names one segment by its id and every other part, in every row, steps back to --ds-chart-dim-opacity - the same input and the same fade the series marks use, so a breakdown filtered by one part can show which part it is. It says nothing to a screen reader: the control that made the choice is what announces it.

@for (part of segmentKey; track part.id) { }

A second reading per row

A row often encodes a ratio and the bar can only draw one of its two numbers. detail prints the other under the label, in the row's own words — it is already formatted, because the row knows what its second number means and the chart does not. It reaches the data table as well.

Share of the total

partOfWhole says the rows add up to something a reader can name — a budget, a headcount, a quarter's revenue. Only then does the chart state each row's share of that total, on the tooltip's second line and as a Share of total column in the data table. It is off by default because rows that are a funnel or a time series sum to a number nobody can point at, and a percentage of that is arithmetic dressed as a fact. Note that the percentage printed beside each row is a different figure: that one is the share of the LARGEST row.

Pinning a reading

A click holds the row and its tooltip in place, so the reading is still on screen after the pointer has gone. A pin disc marks the held row, and an Unpin control appears at the top end of the chart, on the caption's line. Esc releases it, and so does clicking the row again or a press elsewhere. rowClicked fires either way. Set pinOnClick="false" to keep rows unheld, or DS_CONFIG.chart.pinOnClick: false for every chart in the app.

Percent beside the value

showPercent is on by default and prints each row's share of the LARGEST row beside its value — a quick read of how the rows compare to the biggest one. Turn it off where the rows are too narrow to hold a second figure, or where it would be confused with partOfWhole , which is a share of the sum rather than of the maximum. A row can also carry its own percent , which is printed instead of the computed one.

showPercent = true (default)
showPercent = false

Interaction

interactive turns on the hover tint and makes each row a tab stop. The pointer cursor shows wherever a click does something: on an interactive row, and on any row a click would hold. rowClicked fires whether or not the flag is set. dimOthers fades every row but the one the chart is answering: the hovered row once the pointer has rested on it, or the focused one. It is off by default, because the fade costs the separation BETWEEN the faded rows. Resting the pointer on a row's label fades the other rows whether or not dimOthers is set, the way a legend entry singles out a series.

Stagger

staggerMs spaces the rows' arrivals out rather than growing them all at once. 40 is the default, the whole spread fits inside 260ms, and 0 turns it off.

staggerMs = 0
staggerMs = 40 (default)

Data table

none , the default, renders no table at all; toggle puts one behind a closed disclosure; visible renders it open.
