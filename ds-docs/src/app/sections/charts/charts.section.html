Horizontal bars: each row draws its value as a share of the track's maximum.

Default

Rows keep the order you pass them; the chart never sorts. Track length and the printed percent are shares of max : the input when set, else the largest bar end, target or band.

Floating bars

A row's value can be a [low, high] pair instead of one number, and the bar then spans between its own two ends rather than from zero. It is what compares a duration and its PLACEMENT at once - a phase, a shift, a min-to-max - which a bar from zero cannot do. The ends may be written either way round.

Target and bands

target draws a tick across the bar at one reading (a goal, a budget, last period); ranges paints qualitative bands behind it, given as upper bounds. Together they make a bullet chart. Bands use one neutral hue at rising opacity so they stay a backdrop to the bar.

Row colour

Every row takes the same single-series colour; a row that sets color opts out.

Segmented rows

A row that carries segments divides its bar into parts: the bar keeps the row's length, the parts show its make-up. Parts are sized against their own sum and always fill the bar; a sum that differs from value is a data error, not a gap. They take the categorical palette in order unless a segment sets its own color ; the row's colour is unused. Each part reaches the data table as a nested row, and the tooltip lists each part with its share of the row. Parts carry no text, so draw a key from the same --ds-chart-series-N-color values in segment order.

    @for (part of segmentKey; track part.label) {

    • }

Singling out a segment

highlighted names one segment by its id and every other part, in every row, steps back to --ds-chart-dim-opacity - the same input and the same fade the series marks use, so a breakdown filtered by one part can show which part it is. It says nothing to a screen reader: the control that made the choice is what announces it.

@for (part of segmentKey; track part.id) { }

A second reading per row

A row often encodes a ratio and the bar can only draw one of its two numbers. detail prints the other under the label, in the row's own words — it is already formatted, because the row knows what its second number means and the chart does not. It reaches the data table as well.

Share of the total

partOfWhole declares that the rows add up to a nameable total (a budget, a headcount); only then does the chart print each row's share of it, on the tooltip's second line and as a Share of total table column. It is off by default because a funnel or a time series sums to nothing real. The percent beside each row is a different figure: the share of max .

Pinning a reading

A click holds the row and its tooltip in place, so the reading is still on screen after the pointer has gone. A pin disc marks the held row, and an Unpin control appears at the top end of the chart, on the caption's line. Esc releases it, and so does clicking the row again or a press elsewhere. rowClicked fires either way. Set pinOnClick="false" to keep rows unheld, or DS_CONFIG.chart.pinOnClick: false for every chart in the app.

Percent beside the value

showPercent is on by default and prints each row's share of max beside its value. Turn it off where rows are too narrow for a second figure, or where it would be confused with partOfWhole , a share of the sum. A row can also carry its own percent , which is printed instead of the computed one.

showPercent = true (default)
showPercent = false

Interaction

interactive turns on the hover tint and makes the rows one tab stop, with the rows' role switching from img to a group of buttons. Arrow keys move between rows (wrapping), Home and End jump to the ends, Enter or Space fires rowClicked. The pointer cursor shows wherever a click does something: on an interactive row, and on any row a click would hold. rowClicked fires whether or not the flag is set. dimOthers fades every row but the one the chart is answering: the hovered row, its label included, or the focused one. It is off by default, because the fade costs the separation BETWEEN the faded rows.

Stagger

staggerMs spaces the rows' arrivals out rather than growing them all at once. 40 is the default, the whole spread fits inside 260ms, and 0 turns it off.

staggerMs = 0
staggerMs = 40 (default)

Data table

none , the default, renders no table at all; toggle puts one behind a closed disclosure; visible renders it open.

Show
