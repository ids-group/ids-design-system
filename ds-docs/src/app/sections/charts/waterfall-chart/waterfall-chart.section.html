Default

Each bar carries a value that is added to the running total wherever it sits. A delta , the default, hangs from the level the bar before it left off at; a total stands on zero at the sum reached, which is what makes it a figure in its own right rather than a change. A closing total usually carries value: 0 : it prints the sum, not a change of its own.

Colour never carries it alone

Three meanings, three colours - and none of them is load-bearing on its own. Every bar prints its own sign above it, and every bar's accessible name says "increase", "decrease" or "total" in words, so the chart survives forced colours, a monochrome print and a reader who cannot tell the two hues apart. The pair points at the palette's own lead hues rather than at --success and --error : a cost rising is a rise and a bad one, and a hue cannot say which direction is the welcome one.

Subtotals

A subtotal stands on zero like a total, but the chain carries on past it. That is the whole difference between the two.

Crossing the baseline

A chain that falls past zero opens the axis downwards rather than clipping, and the dashed connector stays at the level the two bars share whichever side of the baseline that is.
