The container the column chart is built inside. It owns the plot, the axes, the grid, the value domain and the loading, empty and error states, and draws no marks of its own - reach for it directly when no preset offers the combination you need.

Composing a chart

A mark is an ATTRIBUTE on a real <svg:g> - dsColumnSeries , dsLineSeries , dsReferenceLine , dsReferenceLabel - because an element the SVG specification does not know renders nothing and neither do its children. The svg: prefix is Angular's, and a projected SVG element without it is rejected at compile time.

The container has three slots and they are layers rather than an ordering preference: a reference line paints UNDER the marks whatever order you wrote it in, the default slot takes the marks, and a reference label paints OVER them, so an annotation's stroke stays furniture and its name survives a column drawn through it. Inside the default slot the marks paint in the order they are written.

This one is a column series with a target band behind it and the band's names over it. ds-column-chart wraps exactly this arrangement behind its own thresholds input; written out, that input is what it builds.

Category cursor

cursor="category" draws a band and a crosshair down the category under the pointer, and reports that category to every mark through the shared scales. The default, cursor="auto" , turns it on for a plot of two or more marks that show a tooltip, whose panel then names every mark's reading at the category wherever in it the pointer is. A plot of one mark stays off: its marks are themselves the hover targets and already answer per mark, and two affordances on one plot read as two different questions. A mark set to tooltip="axis" reads that same category and names every series at it.

Under the cursor the category's band is the one hover area: the pointer anywhere in it opens the band and the tooltip together, on a plot of one mark as on a composed one, and the gap between two bands answers nothing. A mark inside the band opens no panel of its own on hover and does not pin on click. Focus opens the category's band and its panel, naming every mark's reading there, pointClicked still fires, and cursorPin holds the category.

Two value axes

A mark declares which axis it is measured against - axis: 'secondary' in a line series' data, axis="secondary" on a column series - and secondaryAxisMode decides how that axis relates to the first. 'aligned' , the default, hangs it on the first axis's own gridlines at a round step, so both marks share one plot; 'split' gives up on sharing and draws two plots over the same category axis. axisColor and secondaryAxisColor paint each axis's labels in the colour of the marks read against it, which is the only thing on the canvas that says which numbers a mark answers to.

Either mark can take either side. Here the columns read the right-hand axis and the line the left, which is the arrangement to keep when the screen being replaced put them that way round.

The dressed version of the single-plot case - both marks answering the pointer, a cumulative line over the columns it sums - is on the column chart page under "Columns and a line, on two scales". It is kept there rather than moved here because a reader arrives at it while reading about columns, which is where the question is asked.

Annotations

dsSeriesAnnotation pins a label to one reading, so the point it names is legible with no pointer - in a printed page, or a screenshot. It takes the readings rather than finding them: a mark never sees the other marks, so the caller that owns the series is the one that knows which value a label belongs to. It reports no domain, because the series it annotates already did.

The annex table

ds-chart-data-table is the annex every chart puts under its plot: each reading in a details a reader can open. Cells arrive already formatted, because how a value reads is the chart's decision and not the table's. rowHeader renders each row's first cell as a th scope="row" , for a matrix whose rows are named rather than numbered, and toggled fires when the disclosure changes - which is how the scatter chart builds twelve thousand rows only once they are asked for.

Naming the axes, and holding the cursor

axisLabel and secondaryAxisLabel name the unit each set of ticks counts, rotated down its own gutter. maxSecondary pins the second axis's ceiling the way max pins the first, so two plots read against each other do not rescale under one another. A press holds the category cursor where it is, by default, with a pin disc at the foot of its band: press again, Escape, or a press outside or between two bands to let go. Set cursorPin="false" to keep it moving with the pointer. A composed chart draws no Unpin control; cursorPinned() and releaseCursor() let you render your own.

Without the category cursor, resting the pointer on a category's label on the axis answers for its column: the column's tooltip opens and every other column dims, whatever dimOthers says, the way a legend entry singles out its series.

The tooltip and the legend of a composed chart

The container opens the one tooltip its marks describe: a mark hands it what the panel shows, so nothing is bound on the mark itself. tooltipTemplate replaces the panel's body and receives the ChartTooltipContext - the category and one entry per series, each already through the chart's valueFormat . tooltipExtraGap pushes the panel further from the mark, for a plot that prints value labels in the gap it would take.

A legend is yours to place: ds-chart-legend 's highlighted feeds the column series' highlighted , matched against its seriesKey , and the line series' highlighted , matched against its series ids. Hovering a legend entry fades the rest to --ds-chart-dim-opacity whether or not dimOthers is set. A line series sits on a band of the surface colour - outline , on by default - so it stays legible where it crosses the columns; switch it off here to compare.

What a line series does on its own

These are the mark's inputs, not the preset's - reach for them once ds-line-chart stops fitting. highlighted names the one series to single out and fades the rest whatever dimOthers says - dimOthers governs only the fade a pointer over a marker starts.
