A ring that splits one total into its parts. Each slice carries its share as a whole percentage and the percentages always add up to exactly 100, so a legend never visibly loses a point. The middle of the ring is free for the total or for a headline figure of your own.

Default

Slices run clockwise from twelve o'clock in the order you pass them and take palette colours in that same order. The shares are whole numbers settled by largest remainder rather than rounded one at a time, which is what makes them total 100 — and why a slice can print a percentage one off the value you would get by rounding it yourself.

Legend placement

The ring alone names nothing, so the legend is the chart's only labelling. end is the default and the widest; bottom is for a narrow column, where the ring would otherwise shrink to make room beside it; none is only safe where the parts are named elsewhere — in an open data table, or in the copy beside the chart.

Ring thickness

The band's width as a fraction of the radius, 0.32 by default. 1 closes the hole and gives you a pie. The centre value still prints there, over the slices rather than over a hole, so it carries a halo of the chart's own background — no single text colour is readable against three different hues.

Centre content

The middle always prints something: your centerValue when you give one, the formatted total when you do not. There is no empty-hole state to design around. centerLabel adds the caption under it, and is what turns a bare number into a figure a reader can name.

Palette slots

A slice takes the palette colour at its position in the list, so dropping one recolours everything after it. Setting slot pins a slice to a fixed palette position instead — the way to keep a category the same colour across two charts that do not hold the same slices. Slots count from one and wrap past the eighth.

Data table

The plot is one image with a generated description, so the annex table is not what makes the chart readable — it is what makes the figures selectable. toggle , the default, leaves the disclosure closed; visible renders it open; none leaves it out.

Edge cases

A ring is a part-to-whole picture, so anything that is not a positive number counts as zero: a negative slice keeps its legend row and draws no arc at all. Past the eighth slice the palette starts over, which is the point at which the data has outgrown a donut.
