Password rules: one bug wearing a design complaint's clothes

The complaint was: cramped, crooked, "not met" written on every row, and the two-column mode "просто пиздец". Three of those four were the same defect, and it was not a spacing decision.

Root cause. The component renders a screen-reader-only span per row (— not met) and hides it with class="visually-hidden". Nothing defined that class. ds-table, ds-date-picker and ds-stepper each declared their own byte-identical copy inside their own stylesheet, so the class worked in those three and silently did nothing here — measured position: static, width: 77.82px. So the text printed. And because it took 78px of every row, the two-column layout ran out of width and wrapped each label onto two lines — which is exactly what read as "скомкано". Fixed and shipped: one shared mixin behind all four. At a true 412px the list now renders four 17px rows, no wrapping, in either mode.

What the named reference actually contains

The pinkmoon password matcher was cited for its "animations, transitions, spacing, states". Read in full, it is plainer than ours, not richer: a flex column, gap: var(--space-1) — the same 4px we use — font-size-2xs (11px, one step SMALLER than our 12px), a colour swap on pass/fail, and no transition or animation of any kind. Reporting that rather than inventing agreement: there is nothing there to copy on motion or density.

It does have one thing worth taking. Its glyph is always a check — check-circle once the rule passes, plain check before that. Ours is a minus while pending, which at 14px beside 12px text reads as a stray em-dash rather than an unticked box.

Call 3 — motion when a rule flips

Today a rule snaps from grey to green the instant the character is typed. A short colour transition is the one place motion earns its keep here: it tells the reader WHICH row changed, which is the thing you cannot see when your eyes are on the field you are typing in. Neither our component nor pinkmoon's has one.
