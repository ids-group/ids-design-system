What the wash costs and buys. The solid pill is unmistakable at a glance and survives a squint; the wash is quieter, which is what both teams wanted in a rail that is on screen all day next to real content. The wash's label carries the state at #1D43ED on #F0F3FF, and the active row stops being the loudest object on the page.

The evidence splits the question in two, and the two halves point opposite ways.

On the fill, the apps are right and we are the outlier. Neither Material nor Carbon fills the active item with the brand accent; both use a quiet selected wash with a coloured or bolder label. Two teams reached for it independently because it is what the rest of the industry does.

On the second signal, the apps are wrong. Carbon keeps a 3px bar AND bumps the weight to 600; Material's wash is named an indicator. The apps turned our rail off, so their active state is carried by colour alone — which is exactly what the ui-ux-pro-max rule nav-state-active warns against: it lists colour, weight, indicator, plural.

So the third column above is the recommendation: take the wash and the coloured label, and KEEP the rail. That would make the DS default match the industry on the fill and beat both consumer overrides on the signal — and both apps could then delete their override instead of extending it.
Four separate verdicts, not one. 1–3 are single tokens and reversible; 4 changes how every app using the sidenav reads. Each is a DEFAULT change, so it is breaking-by-appearance for anyone who did NOT override — which is why none of it has been touched.
