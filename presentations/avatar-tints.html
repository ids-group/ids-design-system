Avatar tints: the named reference is the one that fails

The complaint. The seeded tints read as dull and dark, and every set of initials comes out black. The reference offered was pinkmoon's own seed→colour generator — "there everything was bright and pleasant" — with the fallback "if the auto-contrast can't be made reliable, drop it and pin a token colour".

The auto-contrast is not broken. Measured across the hue circle at the shipped oklch(62% 0.14 h): every hue's relative luminance lands between 0.218 and 0.271, and the black/white crossover is at 0.179 — so black is genuinely the better pick for all of them, at 5.36–6.45:1 where white would give 3.6–4.0. The switch is right; it just never gets to switch, because the LIGHTNESS was chosen just above the crossover for every hue. "Always black" is a symptom of 62%, not of a broken rule.
And pinkmoon's recipe, read in full, is hsl(h 50% 62%) with fg: '#fff' pinned — no auto-contrast at all. It looks brighter because it is lighter, and white text on a light fill does not read: measured across its own 20–300° range, 29 of 29 hues fail WCAG AA, worst 1.65:1 at hue 60. So it cannot be copied — but its INSTINCT (white initials) is right, and gets there by darkening the fill instead of lightening it.

Why no candidate sits between 55% and 60% lightness. That band straddles the crossover, so some hues take white initials and their neighbours take black — a row of avatars with two different text colours, which is the one outcome that genuinely looks broken. Any recipe has to land clearly on one side of 0.179 luminance, never near it.
What needs deciding
  1. Which recipe — A (leave it), B, C or D? E is out on measurement, not taste.
  2. Keep the auto-contrast, or pin the text colour? Once a recipe sits clearly on one side of the crossover the auto rule always returns the same answer, so pinning is equivalent and simpler to reason about — but auto keeps a consumer's own lightness/chroma override readable, which a pinned colour would not.
