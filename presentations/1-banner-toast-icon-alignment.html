Banner & toast — where does the icon sit?

Same content, same tokens, two rules. A is what ships today: the icon aligns to the first line of text. B centres it on the whole message block. Measured on the current build: the icon sits 12px above the block centre at two lines and 21px at three — so the two rules agree on one line and diverge as the text grows.

What the choice actually costs, so it is not decided on one screenshot.
  • They agree on one line. Every single-line banner and the majority of toasts look identical either way — the disagreement only starts at two lines.
  • A is stable under reflow. The icon never moves as text wraps; on a phone the same message is often one line in landscape and three in portrait, and under B the icon slides down as it rewraps.
  • With a title, A ties the icon to the heading — which is the decision `layout-row` already records in a comment (`.icon { align-self: flex-start }`, "icon pins to the title line"). Under B a title-plus-three-lines banner puts the icon beside the body text instead of beside the title it belongs to.
  • B reads better with no title and a long body — the case in the screenshot. There is no first line for the icon to belong to, so top-alignment leaves it hanging in the corner.
  • A third option exists and is not drawn here because it needs a decision first: centre only when there is no title. It gets both wins, at the cost of the same component behaving two ways.
